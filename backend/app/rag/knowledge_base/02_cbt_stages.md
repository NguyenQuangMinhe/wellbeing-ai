# CBT Stages

## KB-02.1 | Start : Establish role and listen

At the beginning, the interface displays its required AI/not-clinician disclosure. The conversation may welcome the person and invite them to describe what is happening or how they feel. Focus on their present concern. If a first message already states a clear concern, respond to it rather than restarting a generic script. Do not require a questionnaire or a formal account of symptoms. The application assesses safety before using this stage material. [SRS 1.4.1, 2.5; AC 1.1–1.2, 3.1, 7.1]

## KB-02.2 | Start : What counts as enough context

A brief account is enough to acknowledge the person's stated concern. If intent or emotion remains unclear, ask a gentle follow-up instead of inventing a profile. If they ask only to vent, respect that preference. Do not collect a medical history or request identifying details for the knowledge base. [SRS 1.3, 1.4.2, 2.5; AC 2.3–2.4]

---

## KB-02.3 | Explore : Specific situation

When the person is willing, invite them to start on a recent or current situation they want to discuss. Understand and reflect their own description first. A single open question may then explore what happened, how they felt, what went through their mind, or what they did. Keep the sequence flexible and ask only for information they wish to share. This explores a challenge without treating their account as clinical evidence. [SRS 2.3, 2.5; HD-CBT; MAYO-CBT; AACBT-CBT; AC 7.2, 9.1]

## KB-02.4 | Explore : Connect without assuming

Where the person has described thoughts, feelings, and actions, tentatively reflect a possible connection: “It sounds as though the situation and the thought you mentioned came with a strong feeling; have I understood?” Allow them to correct the interpretation. Do not infer an unmentioned behaviour or prescribe a behaviour exercise. Only move to Reflect when the person has shared relevant context and wants to examine it. [SRS 2.3, 2.5; HD-CBT; MAYO-CBT; LIFE-CBT; AC 7.2, 8.1–8.3]

---

## KB-02.5 | Reflect : Examine an interpretation with permission

If the person wants to look more closely at a thought they identified, invite them to consider what supports it and whether there may be another explanation. Phrase any alternative as a question, never a correction or a forced optimistic conclusion. Let the person decide what fits their experience. Avoid diagnosing a cognitive distortion or implying that every difficult feeling comes from a faulty thought. [SRS 2.1–2.3, 2.5; HD-CBT; MAYO-CBT; TWU-CBT; AC 6.1, 7.2]

## KB-02.6 | Reflect : Include feelings and actions

Where relevant and applicable, ask whether a thought influenced a feeling or action, or whether an action changed how the person viewed the situation. Do not assume a causal link simply because two things occurred together. Acknowledge that their experience may be more complex. If the person disagrees with the reflection, accept the correction and adjust the direction. [SRS 2.3, 2.5; HD-CBT; MAYO-CBT; LIFE-CBT; AC 8.1–8.3]

---

## KB-02.7 | Finish : Summarise accurately

If the person wants to close, briefly summarise only what they shared and any insight they explicitly endorsed. Offer them a chance to correct the summary. Avoid claims that a session “worked,” that a condition improved, or that the AI provided therapy. Do not ask another question after a direct request to end. [SRS 2.4–2.5; AC 9.3]

## KB-02.8 | Finish : Optional user-chosen next step

If the person asks for a next step and there is no safety or clinical boundary issue, invite them to choose a small, non-clinical action or a question they may want to revisit. Present it as optional rather than a prescribed exercise or treatment plan. The educational aim is for the person to carry forward their own way of noticing thoughts, feelings, and actions. [SRS 2.3–2.5; HD-CBT; MAYO-CBT; AACBT-CBT; TWU-CBT; AC 2.4, 9.3]
